Why Cairn

Above the treeline the trail stops being a trail. It becomes a suggestion — rock, fog, the occasional patch of gravel that might be a path. What keeps you from wandering off is a pile of stones somebody stacked before you arrived.

Nobody owns a cairn. Nobody collects a fee. A stranger you will never meet spent twenty minutes of their one life balancing rocks so that you, specifically, would not get lost.

That seemed like the right shape for a blog.

So this is a cairn too — smaller, slower, made of words instead of granite. Field notes, gotchas, half-finished thoughts, stacked one on top of another as they come. If one of them ever marks the trail for you, the pile has done its job.

The next storm will knock some of it down. That is fine. That is what stones are for — restacking.
